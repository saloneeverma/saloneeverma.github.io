As AI systems increasingly participate in complex decision-making, effective human-AI (HAI) collaboration requires more than accurate recommendations or calibrated reliance. Humans and AI systems must coordinate goals, communicate uncertainty, share control, and adapt over repeated interactions. Yet, existing HAI decision-making research often studies these mechanisms separately, limiting our understanding of HAI as a dynamic team process. This survey examines human-AI joint decision-making through the lens of human-inspired collaborative intelligence. Drawing on human team decision-making, cognitive science, HCI, robotics, and AI, we synthesize work on mutual modeling, communication and alignment, control allocation, and team-level evaluation. We introduce a task taxonomy organized by increasing coordination demand and show that dynamic team tasks remain underdeveloped in current HAI research. Based on this synthesis, we propose a Human-AI Joint Decision-Making framework as a literature-grounded conceptual framework that characterizes HAI decision-making as an adaptive cycle linking mutual modeling, bidirectional communication, control sharing, and team-utility-based evaluation. We conclude with open challenges for building robust, adaptive, and accountable human-AI teams.
